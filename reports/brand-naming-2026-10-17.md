# Startup naming review

Research date: October 17, 2026 (America/Los_Angeles).

## Recommendation

**Takeworthy** — **Human skill. Captured.**

Pronunciation: TAKE-wur-thee. Recommended as a single company and product brand, subject to formal clearance and registrar checkout. The name links a recorded take with work worth learning from. Its strongest weakness is a possible video-production association. Use an explicit robotics descriptor at launch.

Suggested descriptor: Webcam demonstrations. LeRobot datasets.

## Naming strategy and positioning

Audience: university robotics labs and early robotics startups using low-cost arms and LeRobot. The commercial objective is more usable demonstrations per dollar and per hour. Capture convenience is the initial advantage; quality and reusable human skill are the longer-term brand territory.

Positioning: For robotics labs and startups that need demonstration data without a complex capture setup, Takeworthy is software designed to turn ordinary webcam-guided demonstrations into LeRobot datasets, making human skill easier to capture, inspect, and use in robot learning.

This is intended positioning, not a claim that all planned capabilities have been demonstrated. Avoid “any webcam,” guaranteed setup times, autonomous learning claims, or data-quality certification until supported by validation. The reported 90% discard rate and $28–200/hour vendor range were supplied as research leads and were not verified in this naming review.

Use one brand for company and product initially. Keep cameras, arms, and teaching out of the core name. Product modules could later use plain descriptors such as Takeworthy Capture, Takeworthy Datasets, and Takeworthy Quality, when those offerings exist.

Voice: approachable and precise; short explanations, visible measurements, modest claims, and clear distinctions between capture, teleoperation, and learned behavior.

## Candidate scoring

M = memorability; P = pronunciation; D = linguistic distinctiveness; I = ideal-customer resonance; S = scalability. Each is scored 1–5, equally weighted, total /25. These are editorial judgments, not consumer research or probability estimates. Linguistic distinctiveness is not legal clearance. Names found in existing use remain in the longlist to document why they were rejected.

### Preliminary trademark concerns

This was an indexed-web/common-use screen, not a completed search of live and pending records in the USPTO trademark database. No finalist is trademark-cleared. Similar spellings, phonetic variants, common-law use, and relevant goods/services still require examination.

- Takeworthy: investigate exact/close marks and whether “worthy” is perceived as laudatory for quality-related software. This is an issue to assess, not a finding of refusal.
- MotorKin: investigate Motor Kin variants and similarity to [MotorK's software brand](https://www.motork.ai/). Related software use is a reason to investigate, not proof of confusion.
- Deftloom: investigate Deft Loom, sound-alikes, software/textile uses, and the registered .com's owner/activity.
- Skillwick: investigate Skill Wick, Skillwik variants, education/software uses, and the registered .com's owner/activity.
- Mimewire: investigate similarity to [LimeWire](https://limewire.com/about), including its software activities. This is the most conspicuous similarity concern among these finalists.

The USPTO explains [strong versus weak marks](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/strong-trademarks), [sound/appearance/meaning and related goods](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/likelihood-confusion), and [laudatory descriptive wording](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/laws/how-satisfy-disclaimer-requirement). Formal review should cover downloadable software, SaaS, and the intended dataset offerings. This review does not establish registrability.

## Recommended identity

**Takeworthy**

**Human skill. Captured.**

A take preserves a person doing something with intent. Takeworthy starts with a simple webcam and a practical goal: help robotics teams turn demonstrations into structured data they can learn from. The name gives the company room to grow from capture software to datasets and quality tools, while keeping the human source of the skill visible.

Launch descriptor: Webcam demonstrations. LeRobot datasets.

Suggested launch copy, once export functionality is validated: “Capture robot demonstrations with an ordinary webcam. Export LeRobot datasets.” Keep future quality scoring and autonomous outcomes explicitly labeled as future work until proven.

## Decision and next validation

Advance Takeworthy first, MotorKin second. Check registrar checkout for Takeworthy before assuming a domain can be acquired. Complete trademark clearance before adopting the name commercially. Test the top three names with 6–8 target lab/startup buyers: hear the name once, spell it, explain the expected product, and recall it later. Use those observations to challenge this recommendation; the scorecard is a decision aid, not measured market response.
